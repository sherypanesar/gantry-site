gSender is great — but it's stuck at your desk

Let's be clear up front: gSender is an excellent piece of software. Sienci built a clean, approachable G-code sender that a lot of hobbyists — including plenty of OpenBuilds and Shapeoko owners — reach for first, and for good reason. UGS and OpenBuilds Control earn their followings too. This isn't a "gSender killer" article. It's about the one thing every one of those tools has in common, and what to do about it.

They all run on a desktop.

gSender, UGS, OpenBuilds Control, CNCjs, bCNC — every serious GRBL sender lives on a laptop or a host PC wired to the machine. That's not a flaw; it's a design choice that makes sense for a full-featured control station. But it means the moment you want to step away from the computer, you lose your read-out and your controls.

The problem a phone solves (and a desktop can't)

Think about the moments in a session where the desktop actually gets in your way:

  • Setting work zero. You want to be at the bit, eyeballing it against the stock, not reaching back to a keyboard three feet away.
  • Watching the first few minutes of a cut. You want to stand where you can see chips and hear the spindle, with a live position read-out in your hand — not glued to a fixed screen.
  • Checking on a long job. You're doing something else in the shop and just want to glance at progress without walking back to the PC.
  • Machines without a nearby computer. A garage MPCNC or a small engraver where setting up a whole laptop station is more hassle than the job is worth.

A phone is the natural tool for all of these. It's already in your pocket, it has a good screen, and you can carry it around the machine.

Companion, not replacement

The healthiest way to think about a mobile sender is as a companion to your desktop setup, not a rival to it. Different tools for different moments:

Moment Best tool
Deep setup, editing settings, big-screen file inspection Your desktop sender (gSender, etc.)
Jogging and zeroing at the machine Phone
Watching a cut from the right angle Phone
Glancing at progress mid-job from across the shop Phone
Recovering a job after a power blip, on the spot Phone

You don't have to pick a side. Keep gSender for what it's great at and add a phone for the times you'd rather not be chained to the desk.

What a good mobile companion looks like

Gantry was built for exactly this role. It gives you, on your phone:

  • A live DRO (machine + work coordinates, feed, spindle, state) and a thumb jog pad.
  • G-code streaming with start / pause / resume / stop, and a real-time 3D toolpath view so you can watch the head move.
  • Run-from-line recovery, auto-reconnect and keep-awake for when a job drops.
  • Probing, a feeds & speeds calculator, and a surfacing wizard for at-the-machine work.

And crucially, it fits your shop two ways: run a small Wi-Fi server on the same PC you already use for gSender, or connect the phone straight to the controller over USB/Bluetooth with no PC — see running a CNC without a laptop.

It's also the anti-SaaS kind of tool: no account, no ads, no tracking, and free to use, with optional one-time packs for advanced features — no subscription.

Try it alongside what you already use

You don't have to change your desktop workflow to add a phone to it. Keep gSender on the bench; put a live DRO and jog pad in your pocket for everything you do standing at the machine. Gantry is free on Google Play and has a built-in simulator, so you can see how the companion role feels before you connect it to your machine.
